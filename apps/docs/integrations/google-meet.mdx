With the Google Meet integration, Huddle creates a Google Meet link on your Google account at the moment you finalize a poll. The link goes into the event, the calendar invite and the confirmation emails, so participants never wait for a link to follow.

## How it works

1. You connect your Google account once, from your settings.
2. When you create a poll, you choose Google Meet as the video call. The poll shows "Google Meet" to participants while voting is open; there is no link yet.
3. When you finalize the poll, Huddle creates a Google Meet meeting space on your Google account and attaches its link to the event.

The meeting space belongs to the Google account you connected, so it follows that account's Meet settings, such as who can join without asking. Huddle does not change those settings.

## Requirements

- A Google account. A personal Google account or a Google Workspace account both work. On Google Workspace, your administrator must allow Google Meet and let you connect third party apps.
- The Google Meet integration must be enabled on the Huddle instance you use. On huddle.dev it is available to all accounts; on a self-hosted instance the administrator turns it on.
- Google Meet works on every plan, on huddle.dev and self-hosted alike; the link is created when the poll is finalized.

You can connect one Google account for Google Meet. To switch to a different Google account, disconnect the current one, then connect the new one.

### If Google Calendar is already connected

Google Calendar and Google Meet are separate connections in Huddle, but they use the same Google sign in. If you connect Google Meet with the Google account you already connected for Google Calendar, Google asks you to approve the Meet permission, and the calendar access you approved earlier is kept. Huddle keeps a single set of tokens for that Google account, covering both the calendar access you approved earlier and the new Meet permission. Your calendar connection is not affected.

You can also connect Google Meet with a different Google account from the one you use for Google Calendar. Each Google account then has its own connection and its own tokens.

### Finalizing the poll

When you finalize the poll and pick the winning time, Huddle:

1. Creates a Google Meet meeting space on your Google account.
2. Attaches the meeting link to the event.
3. Includes the link in the calendar invite (`.ics`) and in the confirmation emails sent to you and to participants.

A meeting space is a reusable Meet room with its own link. It is not a Google Calendar event, so Huddle does not add anything to your Google Calendar through this integration, and the link is not tied to the scheduled time.

If the link cannot be created, for example because the Google connection has expired or was removed, the poll stays open and Huddle shows an error. Reconnect your account in Conferencing settings and finalize again.

### Which account hosts the meeting

The meeting space is created on the Google account of the poll's organizer. If a space member finalizes a poll organized by someone else, the meeting is still created on the organizer's Google account, and the organizer is the host.

## Permissions

When you connect, Huddle asks Google for these permissions:

| Permission | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Create, edit and see information about the Google Meet conferences Huddle creates (`meetings.space.created`) | To create the meeting space when you finalize a poll. |
| See your primary Google Account email address (`userinfo.email`) | To identify the connection and show which account is connected. |
| See your personal info (`userinfo.profile`) | Requested with the email address to identify the account. Huddle does not store your name or profile picture. |

The `meetings.space.created` permission only lets Huddle create meeting spaces and manage the spaces it created itself. Huddle does not read your other meetings, recordings, transcripts, participants, contacts or email, and does not join or record meetings.

## Data Huddle stores

For a connected Google account, Huddle stores:

- Your Google account id and email address, to identify the connection and show it in your settings.
- The access token and refresh token Google issues, encrypted at rest, so Huddle can create meeting spaces on your behalf without asking you to sign in each time. If the same Google account is also connected for Google Calendar, both connections share these tokens.

For each finalized poll, Huddle stores the link and meeting code of the meeting space it created, as part of the event.

Huddle does not store the content of any meeting.

## Removing the app

### From Huddle

1. Open **Settings**, then **Conferencing**.
2. Next to **Google Meet**, click the **More options** button and choose **Disconnect**.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/integrations/google-meet/disconnect-menu.png" alt="The connected Google Meet row with its menu open, showing Disconnect" />
</Frame>

The connection is removed immediately and Huddle stops creating Google Meet links for you. What happens to the tokens depends on whether the same Google account is connected for Google Calendar:

- If it is not, Huddle deletes the tokens it stored for that Google account.
- If it is, Huddle keeps the tokens, because your calendar connection still needs them.

Disconnecting in Huddle does not revoke the access you granted in your Google account, because the same Google access can also serve your calendar connection. To revoke it, remove Huddle from your Google account as described below.

Polls that already chose Google Meet keep that choice but can no longer be finalized with a Google Meet link until an account is connected again. Links Huddle already created keep working.

### From your Google account

To revoke Huddle's access on the Google side:

1. Open the [third party apps and services](https://myaccount.google.com/connections) page of your Google account.
2. Select **Huddle**.
3. Click **Delete all connections you have with Huddle** and confirm.

This revokes every permission Huddle holds on that Google account, including Google Calendar access if you connected it. Google does not tell Huddle, so the connections still appear in your settings until you disconnect them in Huddle as well; Huddle can no longer use them. If you sign in to Huddle with Google, you can still do so; Google asks you to approve Huddle again the next time.

## Troubleshooting

**"Google Meet is not connected" when creating a poll.** Your Huddle account has no Google Meet connection, or it was removed. Connect one in Conferencing settings and choose Google Meet again.

**Finalizing fails with a video call error.** The Google connection has expired, Huddle's access was removed from your Google account, or the Google Meet permission was not selected when you connected. Disconnect and reconnect Google Meet in Conferencing settings, then finalize again. The poll is unchanged until finalizing succeeds.

**Google blocks the connection.** On a Google Workspace account, your administrator may not allow third party apps or may have turned off Google Meet for your account. Ask your administrator to allow Huddle, or connect a different Google account.

**Participants have to ask to join.** Who can join without asking follows the Meet settings of the host's Google account or Workspace organization. The host can admit people from the meeting, or change the meeting access in Meet's host controls.

**The meeting was created on the wrong account.** The meeting is always created on the poll organizer's connected Google account. Check which account is connected in Conferencing settings.
